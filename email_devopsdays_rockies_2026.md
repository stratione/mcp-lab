**Title:** Hands on the Wheel: Governing AI Agents Across Your DevOps Stack with MCP

**Format:** Open to a 30-minute talk or a 5-minute Ignite talk — whatever fits your schedule.

**Audience level:** Intermediate

**Abstract:**
"Just let the AI do it" is a great demo and a terrible production strategy; until you can answer the real question: what can this agent touch, and can I see everything it did? This talk distills lessons from a workshop I ran at DevOpsDays Austin, plus homelab prototyping. The Model Context Protocol (MCP) makes agentic DevOps governable: instead of handing a model a shell, it exposes your systems as typed, scoped, observable tools. One MCP server drives a small DevOps stack: source control, container registries, and dev-to-prod image promotion via the Registry v2 API. Then the part most talks skip, control and visibility: capability switches bound blast radius, schemas constrain inputs, every call is audited, and a human-in-the-loop stop. It runs on a local open-source model (Ollama), tied to no vendor or cloud. The lab is open source and yours to take home, for your team or self-paced learning. Leave with patterns to expose your systems as safe agent tools.

**Why it fits DevOpsDays:**
Most "AI in your pipeline" guidance shows what an agent can do and skips what operators actually need: scoped access, least privilege, and an audit trail. This is a practical, vendor-neutral pattern for governing agents across a real DevOps stack with MCP, an open standard. The whole lab is open source and runs locally — no SaaS or paid model — so attendees take it home, adapt it, and bring it to their teams.
